# 2. PRICING

## Why we price by joint metre, not square foot

A "$8 per sqft" price is not a price. The work inside a square foot varies **2.67×**
across common tile sizes. Two firms quoting the same per-sqft rate can be delivering
work that differs by nearly three times.

Our own tariff, which is one fixed rule, produces effective rates from **$3.72 to
$13.81 per sqft** depending on job size and tile. Anyone quoting a single per-sqft number
for all jobs is either guessing or excluding something — and that is a fair, checkable
thing to say about any quote a customer shows us.

## Colour — free to choose, charge only what's stranded

The cost of colour is not pigment, it is **dead material**. Grout ships in 3 kg tubs and
the manufacturer requires one batch per area, so each extra colour leaves a remainder
that cannot be reused.

| | |
|---|---|
| Colour from the stocked range | **Free** |
| Matched against the customer's actual tile | **Free** |
| Different colour per room | **+$45** (~$48 of stranded grout on a 3-room job) |
| Special-order colour | **+$80** (whole tub bought for one room, never reused) |
| Metallic / glitter | Quoted |
| Batch codes recorded on the job sheet | Free — and it is the customer's proof |

**Colour rule:** aim for a close match **or** a deliberate contrast. Never land in
between — a near-miss is the one result that reads as a mistake.

# 3. MATERIALS AND BUYING

## What we use

**Kerapoxy CQ** as the default for residential bathroom and kitchen work.
**Kerapoxy Easy Design** as the second SKU for joints under ~3 mm.

Why CQ over plain Kerapoxy: plain Kerapoxy is sand-filled with colour in the resin, so
over-washing during cleanup **bleaches the joint**. CQ carries colour in coated quartz,
so it cleans back to its original colour. It is also **non-sag to 10 mm**, and most of a
bathroom regrout is vertical.

## Kerapoxy CQ specification

| | |
|---|---|
| Compressive strength | 49 N/mm² |
| Flexural strength | 38 N/mm² |
| Water absorption | **0.05 g** |
| Abrasion | 147 mm³ |
| Shrinkage | 0.80 mm/m |
| **Density** | **1.60 kg/L** |
| Mix ratio | pre-dosed **9:1** |
| Pot life | 45 min **at 23 °C** |
| Foot traffic | ~24 h |
| Application temp | **+16 to +32 °C** |
| Class | EN 13888 **RG** · ISO 13007-3 · **ANSI A118.3** |
| Packs | **3 kg**; 10 kg in three colours only |

## Three constraints that bite

1. **Joint width ≥3 mm.** The quartz will not pack into finer joints. **Measure the joint
   on survey** — below 3 mm use Easy Design instead.
2. **+32 °C ceiling.** A Singapore bathroom in the afternoon exceeds it. Pot life roughly
   **halves per +10 °C**, so a 45-minute product gives about **22 minutes at 35 °C**.
3. **Pre-dosed 9:1 means no part-mixing without a scale.** Mapei sanctions it in a
   separate document, but it needs weighing.

## Where to buy

**Singapore — direct from Mapei Tuas.** No Mapei epoxy grout exists on Shopee SG, Lazada
SG, Carousell, Horme or Selffix. **No SGD price is published anywhere** — one phone call
closes the biggest gap in our costing.

**Malaysia** — Kerapoxy 5 kg retails RM239–259, about **S$15.10–16.35/kg**.

**Do not plan a causeway run.** Land imports pay **9% GST with no relief threshold** (the
$400 relief is air/postal only), traveller allowances cover personal use not job stock,
and commercial import needs a TradeNet permit. That takes MY retail to **S$16.85–17.82/kg
landed** before fuel, tolls and time. Unless Mapei Tuas quotes above ~S$18/kg it is not
worth it. Also unresolved: whether Kerapoxy is a **UN Class 9** dangerous good in
transport — get the SDS before planning anything cross-border.

## World price check

Developed-market trade prices cluster at **S$18–26/kg** (Australia S$22–25, USA S$25).
India runs S$3–5/kg in 10 kg trade packs, the UK S$38–40/kg in 1.5 kg consumer packs —
mostly a pack-size effect, not quality.

So: **S$18–26/kg is normal.** Above ~S$30/kg is a small-pack premium. **Below ~S$8/kg is
either genuine Indian/Chinese bulk or not a 100%-solids epoxy — count the components.**

## Buying online — don't, for the grout

Two hurdles kill it:

- **Shopee does not issue tax invoices for third-party sellers**, so a GST-registered
  buyer cannot reclaim input tax. A marketplace price must be **more than 8.3% below** the
  authorised price merely to break even.
- Material is only **4–10% of invoice**, so halving it saves $11–$130 per job — while one
  rework costs $331–$1,582. Break-even is a rise in failure probability of just
  **2.7–6.8 percentage points**. Degraded hardener clears that easily, and it fails at
  12–24 months, inside the warranty.

**Use marketplaces freely for consumables** — pads, sponges, buckets, masking, blades.
Never for the grout.

## Direct from China — a false economy

Epoxy resin ships as **UN3082 Class 9**, hardeners typically **Class 8**, so freight only
works above ~500 kg — which conflicts with the 24-month shelf life. An **NEA Hazardous
Substances Licence** may be required. The category has documented adulteration in its own
home market (nonylphenol and hexamethylenediamine added to mask cheap raw materials;
tested flexural strength of 2.7 MPa against a ≥10 MPa requirement). And Singapore's
**Housing and Building Act imposes non-excludable warranties — 2 years, 6 years for major
defects — on us regardless of material origin**, with no recourse against an anonymous
factory.

Besides: **Mapei already manufactures epoxy at Guangzhou.** Buying through Singapore
distribution may already be Chinese-made product, with certification and backing included.

# 4. ON THE JOB

## Method — full re-grout

1. Protect, sheet furniture, set up dust extraction
2. **Rake out to depth** — at least 2× joint width, or ⅔ of joint depth
3. HEPA-vacuum, wash joints, **dry fully**
4. Grout release on porous, unglazed or textured tile **before** grouting
5. Mix → apply diagonally with a hard rubber float → compact fully → tool
6. Emulsification wash, then second wash
7. Silicone to movement joints, perimeters, changes of plane
8. Protect from water for the stated cure period

Removal depth is **our stated workmanship standard, not a code minimum** — it could not
be found as an explicit clause in TCNA or BS 5385. Photograph it; it is invisible at
handover and it is the dominant cause of re-grout failure.

## Batch sizing — the thing that goes wrong

Pot life halves per +10 °C. One applicator can lay:

| Tile | at 23 °C | at 33 °C |
|---|---|---|
| 600×600 | 5.1 m² (0.9 kg) | 2.1 m² (0.4 kg) |
| 300×300 | 3.3 m² (1.2 kg) | 1.4 m² (0.5 kg) |

**Even a 3 kg tub is more than one person can use in a pot life.** A whole-flat job needs
19 separate mixes at 23 °C, 44 at 33 °C. So **part-mixing is the normal case**, and a
**0.1 g scale is mandatory kit.**

Weighing tolerance is ±3% on ratio:

| Mixing | Part B | ±3% | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full 3 kg tub | 300 g | ±9.0 g | 0.1 g |
| Half | 150 g | ±4.5 g | 0.1 g |
| Quarter | 75 g | ±2.3 g | 0.1 g |
| One tenth | 30 g | ±0.9 g | 0.01 g lab scale |

Below a quarter-tub, don't mix without lab-grade weighing.

## Two clean windows — do not confuse them

| | Initial emulsification wash | Haze / residue removal |
|---|---|---|
| When | During or shortly after the working window (~1 h) | **After cure — 24 h to 7 d** |
| What | Water + white pad, takes off the bulk | Dedicated remover on what the first wash missed |
| If missed | Residue hardens on the tile face | Progressively harder; may need stripper |

Quoting one window's timing for the other operation is the error most likely to damage a
customer's tiles.

## Safety — the one that matters

**Part A is an irritant. Part B is corrosive** (severe skin burns). **Both are skin
sensitisers** — and sensitisation is the one that ends careers: irreversible and
dose-independent once established. Gloves are not optional, and "low-viscosity" epoxy is
not safer — the low-molecular-weight diluents are often *more* aggressive sensitisers.

Silica dust from removal is respirable crystalline silica. Use extraction, not a broom.

## HDB rules — unresolved, assume the strict reading

General renovation is **9am–6pm Mon–Sat, none on Sundays or public holidays**. One HDB
document indicates **noisy work is not permitted on Saturdays** either. Power-tool grout
removal is unambiguously noisy work.

**Operating assumption: weekdays only.** Confirm against a current hdb.gov.sg page before
quoting it to a customer. Whether re-grouting alone needs a permit is **unestablished** —
ask HDB directly.

# 5. WHAT NOT TO DO

Things worth talking a customer out of, and why:

- **Grout in a movement joint.** Perimeters, internal corners, floor-to-wall, around
  sanitaryware. These move and *any* rigid grout cracks there. They take silicone. A
  cracked corner three months after a "full regrout" is almost always this.
- **Cement grout in a shower.** Porous by design. It drinks soap, water and body oils, and
  the black you scrub off is growing inside it. In a wet area it is a three-year
  consumable.
- **Grout pens and colour seal.** Painting over failed grout. Peels in weeks, wettest spot
  first.
- **"Epoxy" that comes pre-mixed in a tub.** Real epoxy is two or three parts mixed on
  site against a clock. Pre-mixed means urethane or emulsion — easier, less resistant. It
  may suit the job; it is not what was quoted.
- **Epoxy in joints under ~3 mm.** The quartz bridges and leaves voids you cannot see.
- **Brilliant white in a kitchen.** White epoxy yellows — chemistry, not defect, and it
  happens in windowless rooms.
- **Grouting over drummy or loose tiles.** Grout does not hold a tile down. Stop and say
  so; it is a tiling job, not a grouting job.
- **Unbranded epoxy.** If nobody names it, nobody stands behind it.

## Three certification claims never to make

- **"HACCP-certified grout"** does not exist. EC 852/2004 regulates food *businesses*, not
  products.
- **"Anti-mould certified to ISO 22196"** — ISO 22196 is an *antibacterial* test, says
  nothing about mould (fungal), and building materials are scope-excluded. The honest
  claim is that epoxy is non-absorbent so mould has nothing to feed on.
- **NSF/ANSI 61 for pool or tile grout** — that is a *drinking water* standard. Pools are
  out of scope entirely.

# 6. REMOVAL DIFFICULTY

How hard the existing joint is to take out, relative to aged cement. **All estimates.**

| What's in the joint | Factor | crew-min/m | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh cement, under ~1 yr (BTO) | 0.55 | 0.88 | Soft, friable — crumbles under carbide |
| **Aged cement CG1** | **1.00** | 1.60 | Hard but friable — the reference case |
| Polymer-modified cement CG2 | 1.25 | 2.00 | Polymer film resists crumbling |
| Pre-mixed urethane / polyurea | 1.40 | 2.24 | Rubbery — gums and loads the blade |
| Modified epoxy emulsion A118.8 | 1.60 | 2.56 | Neither failure mode helps |
| **True epoxy RG / A118.3** | **2.20** | 3.52 | Thermoset — must be ground out |
| Furan A118.5 | 2.50 | 4.00 | As epoxy, plus carbon dust |

On a bathroom floor + shower wall (68 m of joint), removal alone runs **1.0 h to 4.0 h** —
about **$257** of labour.

**Epoxy removal differs in kind, not degree:**

1. **It does not crumble.** Cement falls out as powder; a thermoset must be *cut* along its
   whole length, so time and blade wear both scale badly.
2. **It is bonded to the tile edge** — which is what makes it good grout and what makes it
   dangerous to remove. The failure plane can run into the tile. **Removing epoxy can chip
   tiles and turn a grouting job into a tile-replacement job.**

That is why existing epoxy is quoted as an **estimate requiring survey**, not a fixed
price. It is the only case where our instant quote is qualified.

**Blade economics:** a $22 carbide blade lasting 35 m costs $0.63/m; a $40 diamond blade
lasting 120 m costs $0.33/m. **The cheaper blade is the more expensive one per metre** —
and it is slower, so it inflates labour too. Blade choice hits cost twice.

# 7. THE MATHS

## Theorem — joint density

For any edge-to-edge tiling by congruent rectangles where each joint is shared by two
tiles:

```
λ = (L + W) / (L × W)
```

**Independent of laying pattern** — herringbone costs the same as stack bond. Proof: tile
density is 1/(LW); each tile has perimeter 2(L+W); every joint is shared by two tiles, so
joint length per tile is (L+W). Nothing referenced the row offset.

Corollary: among tiles of equal area, **square minimises grouting work** (AM–GM).

## Material quantity

```
kg per m² = ρ × λ × w × d × 10⁻³
```
ρ = 1.60 for Kerapoxy CQ, w and d in mm. Add 15% waste, then round up to whole tubs —
the remainder is real cost.

Material works out at **$0.88 per joint metre**, about **6.5%** of the joint rate.
**Material grade is nearly free** — paying 50% more for a better product costs ~4% of
price, while being the entire basis of the warranty. Never economise on material.

## Lifetime cost — the argument that settles price objections

```
EAC = P × r / (1 − (1+r)⁻ⁿ)
```

| Option | Price | Life | Cost per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cement, redone on failure | $300 | 3 yr | $110.20 |
| **Epoxy** | $488 | 15 yr | **$47.00** |
| Hack and re-tile | $3,500 | 20 yr | $280.80 |

**Epoxy is cheaper per year than the cement cycle at any price below $1,144**, and pays
back against cement in **5.1 years**. The "epoxy is expensive" objection lives in a price
band that does not exist.

## Why the warranty length matters

A warranty only signals quality if it is cheap for a good operator and ruinous for a bad
one. Most grout failures surface at **12–36 months**, so a **2-year warranty separates
nothing** — it is nearly free for anyone. Only 5 years and up carries information. The
reserve costs about **5.4% of price**.

## Why not to A/B test

Detecting a 30% lift on a 5% baseline needs about **7,560 visits**. At our traffic,
testing is not a decision procedure — decide on mechanism, measure quote-to-booking in
monthly cohorts.
