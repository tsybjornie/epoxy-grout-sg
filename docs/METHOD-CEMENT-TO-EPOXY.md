# Method Statement — Cement Grout Out, Epoxy Grout In

The full job, stage by stage. Written to be followed on site.

**Four hold points** are marked ⛔. Do not pass one until it is satisfied. Every serious
re-grout failure traces back to passing a hold point early.

---

## Before you leave the yard

**Materials**

- Kerapoxy CQ in the agreed colour — **enough tubs of the same batch for each area**
  (Mapei requires one batch per area for colour consistency). Record batch numbers.
- Kerapoxy Easy Design instead, if the survey found joints under ~3 mm
- Grout release agent — if tiles are porous, unglazed, textured or anti-slip
- Neutral-cure silicone in matching colour, plus backer rod if joints are deep
- Epoxy haze remover, in case the second wash isn't enough

**Tools**

- Oscillating multi-tool, **carbide-grit** blades (cement) — bring spares, they wear
- Rotary tool with guide for corners and tight spots
- **M-class vacuum** with on-tool extraction. Not a shop vac
- **0.1 g scale** — mandatory, you will be part-mixing
- Mixing paddle and low-speed drill (300–400 rpm)
- Hard rubber epoxy float
- White scrub pads (bring far more than you think — epoxy clogs them almost immediately)
- Two buckets minimum, clean water, sponges, microfibre
- Protection sheeting, masking tape, bin bags
- Spray bottle for dust suppression
- Raking light / torch — for checking compaction and haze
- **FFP3 masks (fit-tested)**, nitrile gloves, eye protection, knee pads

**Check before loading**

- Grout is within its 24-month shelf life. Old hardener gives soft, under-cured joints
- You have the SDS on the van

---

## Stage 0 — Survey and go/no-go ⛔

Do this before anything else, and be willing to walk away.

| Check | If it fails |
|---|---|
| **Tap every tile.** Hollow sound = drummy | **STOP.** Grout does not hold a tile down. This is a tiling job |
| Any loose, cracked or lifting tiles | **STOP.** Same reason |
| Signs of a leak below, or damp on the ceiling underneath | **STOP.** The membrane is the problem, not the grout |
| Structural or substrate cracking running through tiles | **STOP.** Movement will crack new grout too |
| **Measure the joint width** | Under ~3 mm → Easy Design, not CQ |
| Measure joint depth | Determines removal depth target |
| Identify what's actually in the joint | Existing epoxy is a different job — re-quote |
| Tile type and finish | Porous, unglazed or textured → grout release needed |
| Note every movement joint | Perimeters, internal corners, floor-to-wall, around fixtures — these get silicone, **never grout** |

**⛔ HOLD POINT 1: substrate sound, tiles firm, no active leak.** Proceeding past a
drummy tile means the customer pays twice and blames you the second time.

Photograph the before state, including any defect you've flagged.

---

## Stage 1 — Protect

1. Remove or mask fixtures where practical. Cover what stays
2. Sheet the floor along the carry route, not just the work area
3. Mask the outer edge of any surface you are not grouting
4. Tape a bag over the floor trap — grout slurry sets in a trap
5. Set up ventilation. In a closed bathroom you are working with amine hardener
6. Set up extraction and check the vacuum bag/filter before you start

Open a window or run the extractor fan. It cuts dust, cuts amine vapour, and buys you a
few minutes of pot life by keeping the room cooler.

---

## Stage 2 — Remove the cement grout

**Depth target:**

```
remove to at least   2 × joint width
                or   ⅔ of joint depth
                or   the product minimum
whichever is greatest
```

This is our workmanship standard, not a code minimum — but it is the single biggest
determinant of whether the job lasts. **Too shallow and the new epoxy keys into old
cement instead of the tile edge, so the failure plane is the old grout.** It is invisible
at handover. That is exactly why we photograph it.

**Technique:**

1. Fit a fresh carbide-grit blade. A blunt blade generates heat and wanders
2. **Drop the blade flat into the centre of the joint** — never angled against a tile edge
3. Cut a channel down the middle first, at shallow depth
4. Deepen in **progressive passes**. Never chase full depth in one run
5. Move steadily, roughly 25–50 mm per second. Let the blade cut; don't push
6. Only work out to the tile edges once the blade is tracking in its own groove
7. Corners and around fixtures: rotary tool with the guide, not the oscillating blade

**The chipping mechanism is sideways force against the tile edge.** That is the whole
cause. If chipping starts, switch to a **thinner blade and lighter pressure** — not more
force.

**Dust control, non-negotiable:**

- On-tool extraction running, M-class, into the joint you are cutting
- Mist ahead of the blade with the spray bottle for heavier work
- FFP3 on, fitted. A beard defeats it
- Never dry-cut with an unshrouded grinder indoors

**Walls before floors** — debris falls, so do the vertical work first and clear the floor
once.

---

## Stage 3 — Clean and dry ⛔

Epoxy will not bond to dust and will not tolerate water in the joint.

1. **Vacuum every joint**, running the nozzle along the length, not across
2. Wash the joints and tile faces. Scrub out residual cement dust
3. Vacuum again with the wet nozzle to lift standing water out of the joints
4. **Dry fully.** Fans, ventilation, time. A joint that looks dry at the surface can hold
   water at depth — check with a clean cotton bud pushed into the joint
5. Wipe the tile faces clean. Any residue becomes a bond breaker or shows through

**⛔ HOLD POINT 2: joints dry, dust-free, full depth achieved.** Photograph a
cross-section of an open joint next to a tape measure before you close it up. That photo
is the only proof this stage happened.

---

## Stage 4 — Prepare to grout

1. **Grout release** on porous, unglazed, textured or anti-slip tile. Apply to the tile
   face, not into the joint. Skipping this on textured porcelain is how you spend an
   evening removing haze
2. Mask any adjacent finish that would be hard to clean
3. Check surface and ambient temperature. **Kerapoxy CQ wants +16 to +32 °C.** Above
   that, cool the room, work early, or reschedule
4. Fill both buckets with clean water. Cut a stack of white pads in half
5. Plan your sections — see Stage 5. Mark them mentally before you mix

---

## Stage 5 — Mix ⛔

**Work out your batch size first.** Pot life is 45 minutes at 23 °C and roughly **halves
per +10 °C** — about **22 minutes at 35 °C**. One applicator can lay:

| Tile | at 23 °C | at 33 °C |
|---|---|---|
| 600×600 | ~5.1 m² (0.9 kg) | ~2.1 m² (0.4 kg) |
| 300×300 | ~3.3 m² (1.2 kg) | ~1.4 m² (0.5 kg) |

**A whole 3 kg tub is more than one person can use in a pot life.** Part-mixing is the
normal case, not the exception.

**Mixing a full unit:**

1. Empty Part B into Part A completely. **Scrape the hardener container out** — hardener
   left behind is a ratio error
2. Mix at low speed 300–400 rpm for a full **3 minutes**
3. **Scrape the sides and bottom mid-mix**, then mix again. Unmixed material at the wall
   of the tub is the most common cause of soft patches

**Part-mixing (weighed):**

1. Weigh on the **0.1 g scale**. Ratio is **9 parts A : 1 part B by weight**
2. Tolerance is about **±3%**. On a half-tub that's ±4.5 g on Part B; on a quarter-tub
   ±2.3 g. **Below a quarter-tub, don't — you need lab-grade weighing**
3. Stir each part before drawing from it. Filler settles
4. Mix as above — 3 minutes, scrape, mix again

**Why the ratio is not negotiable:** each amine hydrogen consumes exactly one epoxide
group. Off-ratio in *either* direction leaves an incomplete network — soft, tacky, or
brittle. Too much resin leaves unreacted epoxide, which is also the sensitising species.
Too much hardener leaves free amine that migrates to the surface.

**⛔ HOLD POINT 3: ratio weighed, mixed 3 minutes, sides scraped.** Note the mix time.
The pot-life clock starts now.

---

## Stage 6 — Apply

1. Load the hard rubber float. Hold it at about **45° to the joint direction**
2. Work **diagonally across the joints**, never along them — along-the-joint strokes pull
   material back out
3. **Press firmly to compact.** Voids are invisible now and become failures later. Two
   passes at right angles to each other fills reliably
4. Change direction and go over the same area again from the opposite diagonal
5. Strike off the excess with the float held on edge, still diagonal
6. Work only the section you sized in Stage 5. **Do not run ahead of your wash**

On walls, work bottom-up. CQ is non-sag to 10 mm so it will hold, but bottom-up means
falling material lands on ungrouted tile rather than finished joints.

---

## Stage 7 — Tool the joint

Once the section is filled and struck off, tool the joints to a consistent profile before
the first wash. Slightly concave is right — flush is acceptable, proud is not.

Check with a raking light. A void or a slump shows as a shadow.

---

## Stage 8 — First wash (emulsification) ⛔

This is where jobs are won or lost. **The clock from Stage 5 governs everything here.**

1. Start while the grout is still emulsifiable — during or shortly after the working
   window, guided by the product sheet
2. Water and a **white pad**. Work in **circular motions**, not along the joints —
   straight strokes drag grout out
3. **Change pads constantly.** Epoxy clogs a pad in moments; a clogged pad smears rather
   than lifts
4. **Change water constantly.** Dirty water redeposits epoxy across the tile face
5. Follow with a clean damp sponge, **rinsed and wrung between every pass**, working
   diagonally
6. Don't flood. Excess water at this stage washes grout out of the joint and leaves voids
   at the surface

**⛔ HOLD POINT 4: no section left unwashed past its window.** If you are behind, stop
applying and catch the wash up. Hardened residue on a customer's tile face is a
remediation job on a finished surface — the most expensive mistake available on this job.

---

## Stage 9 — Second wash and haze check

1. Second pass with clean water and clean pads, lifting the residual film
2. Dry the tile and **inspect under a raking light** from a low angle — haze is invisible
   looking straight down and obvious from the side
3. If haze remains: this is a **separate operation with a different timing**, not a
   continuation of Stage 8. Epoxy haze remover after cure, per the product sheet, 24 hours
   to 7 days depending on product

**Do not over-wash trying to clear haze.** With plain Kerapoxy over-washing bleaches the
joint. CQ resists this because the colour is in the quartz, but excessive washing still
opens the surface.

---

## Stage 10 — Silicone the movement joints

**After** the grout has cured, not before.

Silicone goes to:

- The perimeter of every floor
- Internal corners and every change of plane (floor-to-wall, wall-to-wall)
- Junctions with sanitaryware, kerbs and thresholds
- Junctions between dissimilar substrates

Neutral-cure silicone. Backer rod where the joint is deep. Tool it once, cleanly.

**Any rigid grout in these positions will crack** — and a cracked corner three months
after a "full re-grout" is the single most common complaint in this trade. It is almost
always this.

---

## Stage 11 — Cure, protect, hand over

1. **No water for the stated cure period.** Light foot traffic ~24 h; full chemical
   resistance takes days — check the product sheet for the actual figures
2. Leave protection down if others are still working in the space
3. Tell the customer, in writing: when they can use it, when they can wash it, and what
   not to clean it with
4. Final clean of the whole area
5. Photograph the finished work

**Handover pack:** before/during/after photographs including the open-joint depth shot,
the **product name and batch numbers**, the written warranty, and the care note.

That batch number is not paperwork for its own sake — it is how colour stays consistent
if a repair is ever needed, and it is the customer's proof of what actually went into
their bathroom. Almost nobody else in this market provides it.

## Quality checkpoints

| Stage | Check | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Tiles tapped | No hollow sound anywhere |
| 2 | Removal depth | ≥ 2× width or ⅔ depth, verified with a probe and photographed |
| 3 | Joint dryness | Cotton bud comes out dry from full depth |
| 3 | Joint cleanliness | No dust visible under torch |
| 5 | Mix ratio | Weighed, within ±3% |
| 5 | Mix time | Full 3 minutes, sides scraped |
| 6 | Compaction | No voids under raking light |
| 7 | Joint profile | Consistent, slightly concave, no slump |
| 9 | Haze | Nothing visible under raking light from a low angle |
| 10 | Movement joints | Silicone, not grout, at every one |

To claim with 95% confidence that fewer than 5% of joints are defective, you have to
inspect **59 joints** and find nothing. A walk-round checking a dozen supports no such
claim — worth knowing before printing one on a certificate.

## What goes wrong, and at which stage

| Symptom | Cause | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| New grout cracks in corners within months | Grouted a movement joint | 10 |
| Grout debonds in sheets after a year | Removal too shallow — keyed into old cement | 2 |
| Soft or tacky patches | Off-ratio mix, or unmixed material from the tub wall | 5 |
| Whole job soft, never fully hardens | Ratio error, or grout past shelf life | 5 / yard |
| Voids at the joint surface | Over-watering during the first wash | 8 |
| Persistent film on tile face | First wash started too late, or dirty water reused | 8 |
| Haze that won't lift | Missed the emulsification window | 8 |
| Patchy colour across one area | Different batches within an area | yard / 11 |
| Joint colour lighter than the sample | Over-washing | 8 / 9 |
| Chipped tile edges | Sideways blade force against the edge | 2 |
| Grout failed to bond, joint damp | Grouted before the joint dried | 3 |

## Realistic timings

A bathroom floor plus shower wall — roughly 68 m of joint, 110 sqft:

| Stage | Time |
|---|---|
| Survey, protect, set up | 45–60 min |
| Removal (aged cement) | ~1.8 h |
| Clean, vacuum, dry | 45 min plus drying time |
| Mix and apply, in sections | ~2 h |
| Washes | folded into the above, section by section |
| Silicone | 30–45 min, after cure |

Call it a **full day** for one crew, with the silicone and final clean often on a second
visit after cure. **Removing existing epoxy instead of cement would roughly double the
removal stage** — which is why that case is quoted only after a survey.

> All timings derive from estimated rates that have never been measured on our own jobs.
> Log actual start and stop times per stage — twenty jobs of real data beats every figure
> in this document.
