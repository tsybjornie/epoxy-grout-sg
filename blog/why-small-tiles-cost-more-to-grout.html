Why Kit-Kat and Mosaic Walls Cost More to Grout: The Joint-Length Math

By CLEANGROUT | July 2026 | 4 min read

Every week someone asks us a fair question: "Why does my small feature wall cost more than my whole bedroom floor?" The answer is not a markup. It is geometry — and because our tariff is published, we can show you the exact math. By the end of this article you will be able to check any grouting quote yourself.

Grout is priced by metres of joint, not square metres of wall

The material being installed is the line between tiles. So the work in a surface is not its area — it is the total length of joint that has to be raked, cleaned, filled flush and finished. Tile size decides how much joint hides in each square metre:

joint metres per m² = (L + W) / (L × W)
where L and W are the tile's length and width in metres

A real example from a 5-room BTO

A living/dining floor of about 30 m² in 600×600 tile carries roughly 100 metres of joint. A single kit-kat feature wall of 4.3 m² carries 114.7 metres — more joint than the entire living room, packed into a surface a fraction of the size. Priced at the same per-metre rate, the small wall legitimately costs more than a bedroom floor. Nothing is being padded; the wall simply is mostly joint.

How to use this when comparing quotes

  • Ask any contractor: how many metres of joint are you pricing? If the quote is a lump sum with no joint length, they have not measured the job.
  • Multiply your tile's numbers through the formula yourself — it takes one calculator minute.
  • A suspiciously cheap feature-wall price usually means a skim coat over the surface rather than joints filled to depth. Skimmed grout fails in sheets within a year.

One upside worth knowing: small-format walls are where grout colour matters most. On a kit-kat wall the grid is the design — a deliberate contrast colour (charcoal on light tile, champagne on dark) is what makes those walls look intentional. Epoxy is the same price in every colour.
