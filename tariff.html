Comparing quotes

Three questions. Ask us them too.

You should get other quotes — we mean that. But a grouting job is invisible the day it finishes and only shows what it is worth two years later, so comparing the numbers alone tells you almost nothing. These three questions do. We answer all of them in writing, before you pay anything.

One

Which product exactly — brand and name?

Ours: Mapei Kerapoxy CQ, Ardex or Sika — two-part, mixed on site against a clock. Brand, colour and batch number go on your certificate with the supplier receipt.

Watch for: just the word "epoxy". If it arrives ready-mixed in a tub — including Mapei Flexcolor CQ, often confused with Kerapoxy CQ — it is not an epoxy. Different material, different price.

Two

How deep is the old grout removed — and will you photograph it?

Ours: at least two-thirds of the joint depth, photographed open before anything goes back in. You get those photos whether or not you ask.

Watch for: no answer, or a skim over the top. New grout keyed into old cement instead of the tile edge comes away in sheets in about a year — and you cannot see it once the job is finished.

Three

Is the number fixed, and how long is the warranty?

Ours: fixed in writing before we start, from the tariff above. Five years, written, any job size.

Watch for: two years — the trade standard, and just short of the 12 to 36 months where bad workmanship actually surfaces. Also site fees and minimum charges that appear after the crew is already in your house.

If a cheaper quote answers all three the same way, take it. That is a genuinely better price for the same work, and we would rather you had it than had a bad job from us at any price.

The real comparison

Cement grout is cheaper. Once.

Sticker price is the wrong comparison for anything you have to buy again. The honest measure is cost per year of service — what each option actually costs to keep a bathroom in usable condition.

Cost per year is the equivalent annual cost at a 5% discount rate — the standard way to compare purchases with different lifespans. Epoxy is the most expensive option on the day and the cheapest one by more than double over its life.

Why it doesn't go black

Cement grout is porous. It drinks water, and mould grows inside it where no amount of scrubbing reaches. Epoxy has effectively zero water absorption — there is nothing for mould to live in, so it stays the colour you chose.

Why waiting gets expensive fast

Open joints let water past the tile into the screed. While it's cosmetic, it's a few hundred dollars. Once it reaches the waterproofing membrane it becomes a hacking job — tiles out, membrane re-laid, tiles back. The jump is not gradual.

Why we won't be your cheapest quote

Across five quotes on one job, the lowest is usually the one that under-scoped it. The only ways out of an underbid are thinner material, shallower removal, or walking off. We'd rather show our arithmetic and lose on price.

Before you commit

Things we will talk you out of.

Some of these cost you money twice. We would rather lose the job than do them, so here they are in advance.

Grout in a movement joint

The perimeter of a floor, internal corners, where floor meets wall, around sanitaryware — these move, and any rigid grout cracks there. They take flexible silicone, not grout of any kind. A cracked corner three months after a "full regrout" is almost always this.

Cement grout in a shower

Cement grout is porous by design — that is not a defect, it is what it is. It drinks soap, water and body oils, and the black you scrub off is growing inside it. Sealing slows it for a while. In a wet area it is a three-year consumable.

Grout pens and colour seal

Paint over failed grout and you have painted over failed grout. It looks right for a few weeks, then peels — usually first where it is wettest. What is underneath keeps doing whatever it was doing.

"Epoxy" that comes ready-mixed in a tub

Real epoxy is two or three parts mixed on site against a clock. Pre-mixed and ready to use — including Mapei Flexcolor CQ, often confused with Kerapoxy CQ — is not an epoxy: easier to apply, less chemical- and stain-resistant. It may be right for your job. It is not what you were quoted.

Epoxy in joints under about 3 mm

The quartz in most epoxy grout is too coarse for a fine joint, so it bridges and leaves voids you cannot see. Fine joints need a different product, not more effort. We measure the joint before choosing.

Brilliant white in a kitchen

White epoxy yellows. It is the chemistry, not a defect, and it happens in rooms with no window at all. Off-white or light grey looks the same on day one and still looks right in year five.

Grouting over drummy or loose tiles

Tap a tile — if it sounds hollow the problem is underneath it, and grout does not hold a tile down. If we find this we tell you and stop. It is a tiling job, not a grouting job, and doing it in the wrong order wastes both.

Unbranded epoxy with no name on the tub

If nobody will tell you what went into your bathroom, nobody is standing behind it either. Ask any contractor — us included — for the product name and the receipt.

Chipped and broken tiles

The conversation we have before we start, not after.

Grout removal runs a blade along the edge of your tiles. Done properly the risk is small, but it is not zero — and some situations carry real risk however careful we are. Here is exactly how we handle it.

We photograph everything first

Before any tool comes out, we photograph the whole area including every chip, crack and scratch already there, and send them to you. Not to protect ourselves — so that neither of us is relying on memory afterwards.

Do you have spare tiles?

This is the question almost nobody asks, and it is the one that matters most. If there is a box in the store room, a chipped tile is a small fix. If there isn't, and the tile is discontinued, it isn't. Tell us before we start.

When the risk is genuinely higher

Existing epoxy, because it bonds to the tile edge and the failure plane can run into the tile. Already-cracked or drummy tiles. Thin large-format tiles. Some natural stone. If we see any of these we tell you the risk before quoting firm, and for existing epoxy we insist on seeing it first.

If we chip a tile, we say so

Immediately, with a photo, on the day. We make good damage we caused — replacing from your spares where they exist, or making it good as well as it can be made where they don't. What we will not do is find it, say nothing, and hope.

What we can't promise

We can't source a tile that is no longer made, and we can't make a replacement tile in a run of aged tiles look untouched — new tile is a different age to everything around it. Where that is the realistic outcome, you should know it before you agree to the work, not after.

We don't hide behind small print

Nothing here is an attempt to sign away responsibility for our own workmanship — Singapore law wouldn't allow that and we wouldn't want it. This is a disclosure of a real risk and a statement of what we do about it.

The work

What actually goes into your joints.

What we actually use

Ardex WA, Sika SikaCeram, Mapei Kerapoxy CQ, Laticrete SpectraLOCK — true two-part epoxy, named on your quote before you commit. The tubs are in your house and the receipt is yours to see. Anyone quoting "premium epoxy" without a product name is leaving themselves room.

The 45-minute rule

Once mixed, epoxy is workable for about 45 minutes, and the haze has to come off the tile face inside that window or it sets permanently on your tiles. That's why we work in controlled sections, and why a rushed epoxy job is worse than no epoxy job.

The warranty, in writing

Five years on every job, whatever the size — covering cracking, shrinkage, discolouration and joint failure in the treated joints. Most of this trade writes one or two years, which expires just before bad workmanship starts to show. That gap is the whole reason to ask.

Straight answers

The things people ask before booking.

What will my place actually cost?

Use the calculator on the homepage — it runs the same tariff we quote from, so it isn't an estimate that moves later. For reference, on 300×300 tile with old hardened grout: a bathroom floor alone is about $499; floor plus shower walls about $1,074; floor plus every wall is a two-to-three day job at around $2,300, because 300×300 walls hold roughly 155 metres of joint to rake out. A full 4-room flat of newly laid tiles needing first-time grouting is around $2,900.

My tiles are new and not grouted yet. Do you do that?

Yes, and it's the cheapest time to do it — around 40% less than re-grouting, because there is no removal stage at all. If your tiler has laid tiles and left the joints open, going epoxy now means you never re-grout that flat. Send us the tile sizes room by room and we'll break the quote down per room.

Why does tile size change the price?

Because you're paying for joints, not floor. A square metre of 600×600 contains 3.33 metres of joint. The same square metre in 300×300 contains 6.67 — double the removal, double the packing, double the tooling. Large format like 1200×600 is the cheapest to grout at 2.5 m per square metre.

Can you match my existing grout colour?

Yes. We bring the colour chart and match against your tile in your lighting, not from a photo. Most people end up one shade darker than the original — that's the colour it will still be in ten years, rather than the colour cement was on day one.

How long, and when can we use the bathroom?

One bathroom is a single day. A full flat is two to three. Walk on the new joints after 24 hours, showers and water after 72 hours, full chemical cure in about 7 days. We work around you — one toilet at a time if you only have one.

Is epoxy ever the wrong choice?

Yes, and we'll say so. Movement joints, internal corners and tile-to-sanitaryware junctions need flexible silicone, not rigid epoxy — epoxy there will crack. Cracked or drummy tiles need fixing first; grout will not hold a loose tile down. And if your real problem is a failed waterproofing membrane, re-grouting is not the fix, and we'll tell you that instead of taking the job.

Someone quoted me much less. What am I missing?

Usually one of four things: single-part "epoxy-look" acrylic instead of true two-part epoxy; skimming new grout over old instead of raking out to depth; walls or the shower area quietly excluded; or a site fee and minimum charge that appear later. The three questions above sort it out in one message — which product, how deep, is the number fixed. If their answers match ours, their price is genuinely better and you should take it.
